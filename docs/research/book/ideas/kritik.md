# Kritik

**Status:** Naming note and conceptual definition
**Last updated:** 2026-05-17

---

## Core Meaning

In Kant, **Kritik** does not mean a hostile review, a complaint, or a merely negative judgment.

It means a disciplined examination of a faculty, form of reasoning, or claim to determine:

- its conditions of legitimacy
- its proper scope
- its limits
- the line beyond which it falls into illusion, misuse, or unwarranted assertion

In this sense, critique is a tribunal.

It asks not only **what does this faculty produce?** but **what is it entitled to claim?**

---

## Kantian Sense

The key model is Kant's **Kritik der reinen Vernunft**.

There, critique does not attack reason from the outside. It examines reason's own powers from within in order to determine:

- what reason can legitimately know
- under what conditions it can know it
- where it exceeds those conditions and generates error

So the basic Kantian meaning of **Kritik** can be stated as:

**An examination of the authority, limits, and lawful use of a power of thought.**

---

## What Kritik Is Not

Kritik is not:

- a review
- a takedown
- a skeptical rejection of everything
- a stylistic or rhetorical opinion

It is closer to adjudication than to commentary.

It does not ask whether something sounds convincing. It asks whether it is justified.

---

## Why The Word Fits The Skill

The proposed Inquiry skill does not merely inspect outputs for obvious mistakes.

It does something stricter: it examines whether an abductive conclusion is actually licensed by the available evidence, the reliability of the source, the warrant connecting evidence to claim, and the recognized limits of the inferential leap.

That is exactly why **Kritik** is the right word.

The skill's task is not to say, "this answer is bad."

Its task is to ask:

- What is being claimed?
- What evidence supports it?
- What source authorizes that evidence?
- What warrant connects the evidence to the claim?
- Where does the inference outrun what the evidence permits?

This is critique in the Kantian sense: not rhetorical judgment, but examination of entitlement.

---

## Relation To Pure Abduction

If the machine is understood as an abductive engine, then **pure abduction** names abduction before it has been disciplined by explicit evidential control.

In that framing, **Kritik** is the act that places abduction before a tribunal.

It does not abolish abduction. It determines:

- when abduction is productive
- when it is merely plausible
- when it is underjustified
- when it has crossed into confabulation

So the conceptual relation is:

- **Abduction** generates hypotheses
- **Kritik** examines their right to count as knowledge

## Canonical Definition

For this project, **Kritik** means:

**A Kantian examination of the legitimacy, scope, and limits of an inferential claim.**

In the specific context of language machines, that general definition becomes:

**Kritik is the disciplined examination of whether a conclusion is actually authorized by the available evidence, its sources, the warrant that connects them, and the limits of the inferential leap.**

---

## Canonical Uses

### Book Form

For the book, the canonical sense should remain philosophical and compact:

**Kritik is the Kantian examination of what abduction is entitled to claim.**

This is the shortest form that preserves the tribunal sense of critique while tying it directly to the book's central problem.

### Inquiry Form

For Inquiry, the canonical operational form should be:

**`kritik` is the skill that audits whether a conclusion is authorized by the available evidence, sources, warrant, counterevidence, and limits of inference within a bounded corpus.**

This version is more explicit because the runtime surface must tell the user what the skill actually does, not only what philosophical tradition it invokes.

### Shared Short Formula

If one sentence is needed across both projects:

**Kritik places a conclusion before a tribunal of evidence, source, warrant, counterevidence, and limit.**

---

## Naming Implication

This makes **Kritik** a stronger name than **Critique** for the skill.

**Critique** is the English translation and works well as the book title.

**Kritik** preserves the explicit Kantian resonance and marks the skill as a disciplined operation rather than a generic review step.

Recommended split:

- **Book title:** Critique of Pure Abduction
- **Skill name:** Kritik
