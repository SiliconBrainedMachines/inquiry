## Who He Is

Geoffrey Hinton (born 1947, London). Cognitive psychologist and computer scientist. Often called the "Godfather of Deep Learning." Shared the 2018 Turing Award with Yann LeCun and Yoshua Bengio. Resigned from Google in 2023 to speak freely about AI risks. Nobel Prize in Physics 2024 (with John Hopfield) for foundational work on artificial neural networks.

---

## Key Contributions

| Year | Contribution | Significance |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| 1986 | Backpropagation (with Rumelhart & Williams) | Made training deep networks practical. The fundamental algorithm behind all modern neural network training. |
| 2006 | Deep Belief Networks | Showed that deep networks could be pre-trained layer by layer, reigniting interest in deep learning after the "AI winter." |
| 2012 | AlexNet (with Krizhevsky & Sutskever) | Won ImageNet competition by a massive margin using deep convolutional neural networks. Proved deep learning works at scale. Catalyzed the current AI revolution. |
| 2017 | Capsule Networks | Proposed an alternative to CNNs that better captures spatial hierarchies (less successful commercially but theoretically important). |

## The Chomsky Motivation

In multiple interviews, Hinton has described his motivation as partly a reaction against Chomsky's nativism:

> "I was also motivated by the fact there is a school of linguistics, the kind of Chomsky school, that claimed that language wasn't learned, that it was all innate. And that seemed to me to be complete rubbish. And so I was motivated to show that they were wrong."

This is significant because:

1. **It reveals AI as a philosophical project.** Hinton didn't just build neural networks for engineering reasons. He built them to answer a philosophical question: *Can language be learned, or must it be innate?*

2. **The motivation preceded the result by decades.** Hinton started working on neural networks in the 1970s. It wasn't until the 2010s–2020s (GPT, BERT, etc.) that machines demonstrably "learned" natural language. The conviction that Chomsky was wrong sustained decades of work.

3. **The result is ambiguous.** Transformers did learn language — but they did so with a *specific architecture* (attention, positional encoding, layer normalization). Whether this counts as "innate structure" (vindicating Chomsky's core claim about the necessity of architecture) or "pure learning" (refuting him) is an open philosophical question.

## Hinton's Position on AI Risk (2023–present)

After leaving Google, Hinton has warned publicly about:
- Existential risk from superintelligent AI
- AI systems that may already understand language in some meaningful sense
- The difficulty of aligning AI goals with human values
- The possibility that digital intelligence may be fundamentally superior to biological intelligence

This connects to **Chapter 3 (Mind)** — Hinton takes seriously the possibility that machines might "understand," which puts him in tension with Searle's Chinese Room.
