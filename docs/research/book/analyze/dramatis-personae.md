# Dramatis Personae

**The witnesses convoked before the tribunal of artificial intelligence.**

Each thinker appears because they illuminate something specific about AI that no one else illuminates. Inclusion follows the selection criterion documented in [risks.md](risks.md): no tokenism, no decoration — only unique insight earns a seat.

**Status:** Working list. Subject to revision during Plan phase research.

---

## By Chapter

### 1. Language — Can a machine mean?

| Thinker | Era | Tradition | Contribution to the Chapter |
|---------|-----|-----------|----------------------------|
| Plato | 4th c. BC | Greek | *Cratylus* — do names reflect the nature of things, or are they arbitrary? |
| Zhuangzi | 4th c. BC | Chinese (Daoist) | The limits of language for capturing reality; the finger pointing at the moon |
| Ferdinand de Saussure | 1857–1913 | Swiss (Structuralism) | Signs are differential — meaning arises from contrast, not correspondence |
| Ludwig Wittgenstein | 1889–1951 | Austrian (Analytic) | Early: language as logical picture. Late: meaning as use in context |
| Jacques Derrida | 1930–2004 | French (Post-structuralism) | Meaning is always deferred (*différance*); no final fixed interpretation |

### 2. Knowledge — Is knowing remembering or computing?

| Thinker | Era | Tradition | Contribution to the Chapter |
|---------|-----|-----------|----------------------------|
| Plato | 4th c. BC | Greek | *Anamnesis* — knowledge as remembering what the soul already knows |
| John Locke | 1632–1704 | English (Empiricism) | *Tabula rasa* — all knowledge comes from sensory experience |
| David Hume | 1711–1776 | Scottish (Empiricism) | The problem of induction — past regularities don't guarantee future ones |
| Immanuel Kant | 1724–1804 | German (Critical Philosophy) | Categories of understanding that structure all possible experience |
| Karl Popper | 1902–1994 | Austrian (Critical Rationalism) | Knowledge advances through falsification, not confirmation |

### 3. Mind — Is anyone in there?

| Thinker | Era | Tradition | Contribution to the Chapter |
|---------|-----|-----------|----------------------------|
| René Descartes | 1596–1650 | French (Rationalism) | *Cogito ergo sum* — the one certainty; mind-body dualism |
| John Searle | 1932–2022 | American (Philosophy of Mind) | Chinese Room — syntax alone is not semantics |
| Thomas Nagel | b. 1937 | American (Philosophy of Mind) | "What is it like to be a bat?" — subjective experience is irreducible |
| David Chalmers | b. 1966 | Australian (Philosophy of Mind) | The hard problem of consciousness — why is there something it is like? |
| Alan Turing | 1912–1954 | British (Mathematics/CS) | The imitation game — can a machine think? The operational test |

### 4. Reason — Deduction, induction, abduction, and the machines

| Thinker | Era | Tradition | Contribution to the Chapter |
|---------|-----|-----------|----------------------------|
| Aristotle | 4th c. BC | Greek | Syllogistic logic, formal categories — the first system of valid inference |
| Charles Sanders Peirce | 1839–1914 | American (Pragmatism) | Abduction — inference to the best explanation. The LLM's native mode |
| Karl Popper | 1902–1994 | Austrian (Critical Rationalism) | The asymmetry between verification and falsification |
| Daniel Kahneman | 1934–2024 | Israeli-American (Psychology) | System 1 / System 2 — fast intuitive vs. slow deliberate thinking |

### 5. Ethics — Who is responsible for what a machine does?

| Thinker | Era | Tradition | Contribution to the Chapter |
|---------|-----|-----------|----------------------------|
| Aristotle | 4th c. BC | Greek | Virtue ethics — character and habits, not just rules |
| Immanuel Kant | 1724–1804 | German (Critical Philosophy) | Categorical imperative — duty-based, universal moral law |
| John Stuart Mill | 1806–1873 | British (Utilitarianism) | Consequences matter most — greatest good for greatest number |
| Emmanuel Levinas | 1906–1995 | French-Lithuanian (Phenomenology) | The face of the other — responsibility as encounter, not calculation |

### 6. Learning — Can you improve without living?

| Thinker | Era | Tradition | Contribution to the Chapter |
|---------|-----|-----------|----------------------------|
| John Dewey | 1859–1952 | American (Pragmatism) | Learning by doing — experience as the basis of all real knowledge |
| Jean Piaget | 1896–1980 | Swiss (Developmental Psychology) | Constructivism — knowledge is built, not received |
| Lev Vygotsky | 1896–1934 | Soviet (Developmental Psychology) | Zone of proximal development — learning with assistance |
| Buddhist tradition | ~5th c. BC onward | Indian (Buddhism) | Dependent origination — nothing learns in isolation; all arising is relational |

### 7. Truth — Can a machine lie?

| Thinker | Era | Tradition | Contribution to the Chapter |
|---------|-----|-----------|----------------------------|
| Parmenides | 5th c. BC | Greek (Presocratic) | Being is; non-being is not. Truth as unchanging reality |
| Heraclitus | 5th c. BC | Greek (Presocratic) | Everything flows — truth as process, not state |
| Thomas Aquinas | 1225–1274 | Italian (Scholasticism) | Truth as *adaequatio* — correspondence between mind and thing |
| Alfred Tarski | 1901–1983 | Polish (Logic/Mathematics) | Formal semantic theory of truth — "snow is white" is true iff snow is white |
| Harry Frankfurt | 1929–2023 | American (Analytic Philosophy) | *On Bullshit* — the distinction between lying (caring about truth) and bullshitting (indifferent to truth). The LLM as structural bullshitter? |

### 8. Freedom — Agency without will?

| Thinker | Era | Tradition | Contribution to the Chapter |
|---------|-----|-----------|----------------------------|
| Stoic school | 3rd c. BC onward | Greek/Roman | *Dichotomy of control* — what is within our power vs. what is not |
| Baruch Spinoza | 1632–1677 | Dutch (Rationalism) | Determinism and the illusion of free will — we are nature thinking |
| Jean-Paul Sartre | 1905–1980 | French (Existentialism) | Radical freedom — we are condemned to be free, even in choosing not to choose |
| Compatibilists | Various | Analytic tradition | Freedom as acting on your desires, even if the universe is determined |

### 9. Beauty — Can the unfelt be created?

| Thinker | Era | Tradition | Contribution to the Chapter |
|---------|-----|-----------|----------------------------|
| Plato | 4th c. BC | Greek | Art as imitation of imitation — twice removed from the real |
| Aristotle | 4th c. BC | Greek | *Catharsis* — art as emotional transformation, not mere representation |
| G.W.F. Hegel | 1770–1831 | German (Idealism) | Art as expression of *Geist* (spirit) — art embodies an era's self-understanding |
| Theodor Adorno | 1903–1969 | German (Critical Theory) | Art after Auschwitz — art's relationship to suffering and the limits of expression |
| Wabi-sabi | Traditional | Japanese (Zen aesthetics) | Beauty in imperfection, transience, incompleteness |
