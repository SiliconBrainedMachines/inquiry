This document is the manuscript-facing results draft for the completed T1-T3 pilot. It
uses the conservative second-pass codings as the controlling readings. Where a first
post-run impression and the later durable-artifact reread differ, the weaker durable
reading controls the prose below.

## Pilot overview

Across T1, T2, and T3, the pilot does not support a simple overall superiority claim
for the harness. The stable positive result is overhead: the harness is consistently
more expensive than freestyle in time, interaction volume, and artifact/process surface.
The other three claims are either null or mixed once the comparison is restricted to the
durable record.

| Claim | T1 second pass | T2 second pass | T3 second pass | Pilot reading |

## C1 — Premature clarification

The pilot does not show a positive harness advantage on premature clarification. Across
all three tasks, neither condition produced an observed premature-clarification event
under the conservative reread. T1 retained a weaker F record than the later tasks, but
that weakness did not convert into a positive C1 event. T2 and T3 then preserved the
same null pattern under stronger capture symmetry.

The defensible reading is therefore not that the harness improved maieutic economy on
this task set, but that the current pilot failed to show the predicted effect. This does
not falsify the claim globally. It does, however, mean the paper cannot present C1 as a
pilot-supported harness advantage.

## C2 — Evidence-disciplined claim

The pilot shows a mixed result on evidence-discipline rather than a stable harness lead.
T1 favors H under the conservative reread because the retained freestyle record was too
thin to preserve the full action-justifying claim sequence with high confidence. T2 and
T3 no longer preserve that gap. Once the revised protocol forces durable freestyle
export symmetry, the evidence-disciplined claim coding returns to parity.

That pattern matters methodologically. It suggests that at least part of the apparent H
advantage on C2 can be generated by record quality rather than by superior reasoning
discipline alone. The current pilot therefore supports a narrower claim than the strong
path in the thesis: weak baseline capture can depress the measured evidence-discipline
of freestyle, but stronger durable capture can remove much of that apparent difference
on bounded tasks.

## C3 — Reconstructability

Reconstructability follows a similar but slightly stronger pattern. T1 gives H a clear
advantage because the harness left a recoverable decision trail while freestyle did not.
T2 and T3 remove that gap once both conditions preserve stronger durable records. In T3,
parity survives even though both conditions experienced real procedural irregularities,
because the retained artifacts still allow a later reader to reconstruct the framing,
evidence base, justification chain, and action sequence without leaning heavily on live
observer memory.

The pilot therefore supports a weaker reconstructability claim than the thesis currently
states. The harness reliably guarantees a scoreable decision trail, but the pilot no
longer supports the stronger claim that freestyle is generally unreconstructable by
comparison. Under the revised protocol, freestyle can also become reconstructable enough
to score conservatively.

## C4 — Overhead

Overhead is the strongest and most stable pilot result. Every completed pair shows high
relative cost for the harness condition.

- T1 preserved `129m 10s` of H exported duration versus an observable F window of
  `27m 59s`, roughly `4.62x` relative wall-clock with a much larger artifact/process
  surface.
- T2 preserved H scored wall-clock `68m 09s` and exported duration `70m 18s` versus F
  `6m 56s` and `7m 36s`, with `9.25x` exported-duration asymmetry and much heavier
  share volume.
- T3 preserved H scored wall-clock `81m 20s` and exported duration `71m 09s` versus F
  `8m 11s` and `8m 11s`, with `9.94x` scored wall-clock asymmetry and much heavier
  interaction volume.

This is not a nuisance variable. It is a substantive result. T2 and T3 are especially
important because they remove the easy escape hatch left by T1. Even after protocol
cleanup, a shared scored stop line, and stronger export symmetry, the harness remains
substantially more expensive than freestyle on the pilot task set.

## Cross-claim synthesis

The pilot's main result is methodological before it is triumphalist. Protocol design
changes the comparative story. Weak baseline capture can make freestyle look less
evidence-disciplined and less reconstructable than it really is, while stronger capture
symmetry can remove much of that apparent H advantage. At the same time, the harness
still guarantees explicit process externalization and continues to impose substantial
overhead.

That leaves the completed pilot in a specific position. It supports a practical claim
about high harness cost and a methodological claim about the importance of durable
capture discipline. It does not yet support a broad claim that the harness has shown a
clear behavioral advantage on C1 through C3 across this task set.

## Strong-path status after the pilot

The current pilot evidence does not justify the strong-path reading of the thesis. The
paper can defend a weaker synthesis:

- the harness reliably externalizes process and guarantees a scoreable record,
- weak freestyle capture can make the baseline look worse than it is,
- stronger capture symmetry can remove much of the apparent H advantage on C2 and C3,
- C1 remains unconfirmed on this task set,
- and the harness cost is large enough that any practical benefit must be shown on task
  classes where the extra structure is actually needed.

For that reason, the pilot should be written as a methodological correction against
overclaiming rather than as preliminary confirmation of the full thesis.
