## Claim

The paper's contribution is not to show that some old philosophical ideas remain useful.
That would be too weak. The contribution is to argue that philosophical lineage can
function as a **source of operator taxonomy for AI engineering**: a disciplined way to
define, separate, and operationalize rules of inference that would otherwise appear only
as generic prompt heuristics [plato_theaetetus; descartes_discourse; dewey_logic;
peirce_cp].

## What is new

Three distinct contributions are claimed.

1. **Taxonomic contribution.** The paper treats named philosophical procedures as
   candidate operator classes rather than as cultural references or abstract doctrines.
2. **Engineering contribution.** It reifies those operator classes as explicit harness
   components over an abductive machine.
3. **Methodological contribution.** It turns operator design into a cumulative empirical
   program: operators can be implemented, ablated, compared, and falsified.

## Why lineage matters

The relevance of philosophical lineage is not prestige. It is that philosophy already
performed long differentiation work on kinds of inferential discipline [plato_theaetetus;
descartes_discourse; dewey_logic; peirce_cp]. The lineage supplies boundaries and
normative semantics: what problem each operator solves, what counts as success, and what
should be withheld. Good scaffolding can imitate surface behavior; lineage helps specify
the operator.

## What the paper does not claim

- philosophy is the only source of useful inference operators,
- every philosophical method survives operationalization,
- or historical pedigree is itself evidence of effectiveness.

## Failure condition

If the named operators cannot be distinguished from generic scaffolding in either
specification or behavior, then the contribution shrinks to historical curiosity. The
paper has to risk that outcome.
