## Claim-by-claim synthesis

## C1 — Premature clarification

### Observed pattern

None of the three pilot tasks shows an observed C1 advantage for the harness. Across
T1, T2, and T3, both conditions move from packet intake into repository inspection and
bounded execution without a premature clarification event.

### Pilot reading

After three pilot tasks, the current evidence does not support a positive C1 claim for
the harness. That does not falsify C1 globally, but it does mean the pilot has not yet
shown the predicted maieutic advantage on this task set.

## C2 — Evidence-disciplined claim

### Observed pattern

- T1 favors H under the conservative reread because the freestyle durable record was too
  thin to preserve the full claim sequence with high confidence.
- T2 returns to parity once the revised protocol preserves enough durable freestyle
  evidence to sustain the same claim-level coding under the stricter rule.
- T3 stays at parity even though both conditions experience real procedural deviations,
  because the retained durable record is still strong enough to preserve the decisive
  claim sequence in both conditions.

### Pilot reading

The three-task pilot does not show a stable harness advantage on C2. The strongest
defensible reading is narrower and methodological:

1. weak freestyle capture can collapse C2 confidence even when the live run looked
   evidence-first,
2. stronger durable capture can repair that measurement problem,
3. and once repaired, bounded tasks may show parity rather than a harness lead.

The pilot therefore leaves C2 mixed, with the weight of the current evidence shifting
toward parity on bounded tasks when the baseline record is made durable enough.

## C3 — Reconstructability

### Observed pattern

- T1 shows a clear H advantage because the harness left a reconstructable decision trail
  while freestyle did not.
- T2 removes that gap once export symmetry is enforced and both runs leave enough durable
  evidence for third-party reconstruction.
- T3 preserves that parity despite relaunches and artifact irregularities, because the
  durable share-plus-artifact bundle remains strong enough for conservative scoring.

### Pilot reading

The pilot supports a weaker reconstructability claim than the thesis currently states.
So far, the harness clearly guarantees a scoreable decision trail, but T2 and T3 show
that freestyle can also become reconstructable when the protocol forces stronger durable
capture.

That makes C3 mixed overall, but with a clear methodological pattern: the apparent H
advantage shrinks sharply once the baseline capture path is no longer weak.

## C4 — Overhead

### Observed pattern

C4 is the most stable result in the pilot.

- T1: H exported duration `129m 10s` versus an observable F window of `27m 59s`, with
  about `4.62x` relative wall-clock and a much larger artifact/process surface.
- T2: H scored wall-clock `68m 09s` and exported duration `70m 18s` versus F `6m 56s`
  and `7m 36s`, with `9.25x` exported-duration asymmetry and much heavier share volume.
- T3: H scored wall-clock `81m 20s` and exported duration `71m 09s` versus F `8m 11s`
  and `8m 11s`, with `9.94x` scored wall-clock asymmetry and much heavier share volume.

### Pilot reading

The pilot strongly supports C4 as currently framed: harness overhead is real,
observable, and large enough that it must be reported as a first-class result rather
than treated as nuisance cost.

T2 and T3 are especially important because they remove the easy escape hatch that T1
left open. Even after protocol cleanup, a shared scored stop line, and stronger export
symmetry, H remains substantially more expensive than F.

## What the three pilot tasks jointly support

After three tasks, the most defensible pilot reading is:

1. the pilot clearly supports the overhead claim,
2. it does not support a positive harness advantage on premature clarification,
3. it shows mixed evidence on evidence-discipline, with the stronger recent pattern at parity,
4. and it shows mixed evidence on reconstructability once freestyle capture is made methodologically stronger.

This is enough to support a methodological conclusion: protocol design changes the
observable comparative story, especially on C2 and C3.

## Strong-path status after T1-T3

The current pilot evidence does not justify a strong-path results claim for the first
paper.

What is currently defensible is a weaker synthesis:

- the harness reliably externalizes process and guarantees a scoreable record,
- weak freestyle capture can make the baseline look less evidence-disciplined and less
  reconstructable than it really is,
- stronger capture symmetry can remove much of the apparent H advantage on C2 and C3,
- C1 remains unconfirmed on this task set,
- and the harness cost remains large enough that any practical benefit must be shown on
  task classes where that extra structure is actually needed.
