This document is the manuscript-facing discussion draft for the first paper. Its job is
to interpret the pilot without reintroducing claims that the results and validity record
do not support.

## The main lesson of the pilot

The strongest lesson of the completed pilot is methodological rather than triumphalist.
The comparison does not show a broad Harness win on all of the paper's predicted
behavioral constructs. What it shows is that protocol design changes the comparative
story. When the baseline capture path is weak, freestyle can look less
evidence-disciplined and less reconstructable than it may really be. When durable
capture symmetry is strengthened, much of that apparent advantage can shrink toward
parity.

That is already an important result. It means that, in this pilot, a thin baseline
record could make a structured harness look stronger than the bounded evidence later
warranted.

## What the pilot does support

The pilot supports three claims with reasonable confidence.

First, Inquiry's harness reliably externalizes process and preserves a scoreable record.
Even where the paired runs become messy, the harness leaves a durable trail rich enough
to support later reconstructive scoring.

Second, the baseline comparison is highly sensitive to record quality. T1 suggested a
clear H advantage on C2 and C3, but T2 and T3 show that once the freestyle record is
made durably comparable, those advantages can narrow substantially.

Third, the harness imposes substantial overhead. This is not a minor qualifier. It is
the most stable positive result in the pilot and must remain central to any practical
interpretation of the system.

## What the pilot does not support

The pilot does not support a strong-path claim that the named philosophical operators
have already demonstrated a clear behavioral advantage over freestyle across this task
set. C1 remains unconfirmed. C2 and C3 are mixed and appear sensitive to capture
discipline. The current evidence therefore cannot cleanly separate operator effect from
generic structure and artifact quality.

This matters because the paper's non-trivial contribution depends on exactly that point.
If the named operators cannot yet be behaviorally distinguished from structured process
and better capture, the paper must not write as if the distinction has already been won.

## Why the narrowed framing is stronger than overclaiming

Narrowing the thesis does not empty the paper of value. It makes the paper more precise.
The current pilot supports a methodological contribution: explicit inference governance
can be studied empirically only if the comparison design preserves durable, comparable
records across conditions. It also supports a practical contribution: the cost of that
governance is large enough that it must be justified by task class rather than assumed
to be worth paying.

That narrower framing is still compatible with the broader research line. It simply
refuses to let an early pilot stand in for a stronger later test.

## Implications for Inquiry as an engineering artifact

For Inquiry itself, the pilot implies two things at once.

On the positive side, the system's core value proposition as a process-externalizing and
trace-preserving harness survives the pilot. On the critical side, that value
proposition is currently easier to defend on reconstructable process and auditability
than on demonstrable superiority of the named operators' behavioral effects.

This means future engineering work should not treat overhead as something to explain away
after the fact. If the harness is to be practically persuasive, it must either reduce
its own workflow cost or show task classes where that extra structure clearly earns its
keep.

## What a stronger next study would need

A stronger next study would need to isolate the strong path more cleanly than the pilot
could. At minimum, it would need:

1. a comparison in which baseline capture quality is durable by design rather than by
   later repair,
2. a way to separate named-operator effect from generic structured scaffolding,
3. task classes where the extra governance structure has a plausible chance to matter,
4. and continued honest reporting of overhead even if the stronger design still shows
   substantial cost.

Until then, the first paper should present the pilot as a disciplined narrowing of the
research claim rather than as a preliminary win for the original strong-path thesis.
