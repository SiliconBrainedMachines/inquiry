# Results Section Scaffold - Pilot Claims

> **Type:** plan
> **Status:** draft
> **Depends on:** [research-question.md](research-question.md), [thesis.md](thesis.md), [evidence/pilot-claim-summary-t1-t2-t3.md](evidence/pilot-claim-summary-t1-t2-t3.md)
> **Used by:** [first-paper-checklist.md](first-paper-checklist.md), future manuscript draft

This file is a writing scaffold for the paper's results section. It is not the final
results prose. Its job is to turn the retained pilot evidence into manuscript-ready
structure without letting the manuscript drift beyond the scored record.

Use the conservative second-pass readings as the controlling pilot scores. If later
drafts cite a first-pass judgment where the second pass disagrees, the prose should be
rewritten to follow the second-pass reading instead.

## Section opening

Suggested opening paragraph:

> The pilot is reported claim by claim using the stricter second-pass codings rather
> than the initial post-run impressions. This keeps the manuscript anchored to durable
> artifacts rather than to live observer memory. Across T1, T2, and T3, the pilot now
> shows a stable overhead result, a null reading on premature clarification, and mixed
> but method-sensitive readings on evidence-discipline and reconstructability.

## C1 - Premature clarification

Suggested paragraph skeleton:

> On C1, the pilot has not yet shown a positive harness advantage. T1 and T2 both lack
> an observed premature-clarification event in either condition under the conservative
> reread, and T3 preserves that same null pattern. The defensible pilot reading is
> therefore that the maieutic claim remains unconfirmed on this task set rather than
> supported.

Write this section as a null-sensitive result. Do not convert lack of observed
premature clarification into a rhetorical H advantage.

## C2 - Evidence-disciplined claim

Suggested paragraph skeleton:

> On C2, the pilot currently shows a mixed pattern rather than a stable harness lead.
> T1 favors H under the durable-artifact-first reread because the freestyle record is
> too thin to preserve the full claim sequence with high confidence. T2 returns to
> parity once the revised protocol preserves durable freestyle evidence, and T3 remains
> at parity even after both conditions experience real procedural deviations. The pilot
> therefore supports at least a methodological claim: record quality materially affects
> whether evidence-discipline can be scored cleanly.

Current writing direction:

- write C2 as mixed and not yet sufficient for a strong-path claim,
- emphasize that the recent pattern is parity once baseline capture is durable,
- keep the methodological point that weak baseline capture can manufacture an apparent H advantage.

## C3 - Reconstructability

Suggested paragraph skeleton:

> On C3, the pilot again shows a mixed pattern. T1 gives H a clear advantage because
> the harness leaves a reconstructable decision trail while freestyle does not. T2
> removes that gap once export symmetry is enforced and both runs leave enough durable
> evidence for third-party reconstruction. T3 preserves that parity despite relaunches
> and artifact irregularities. The pilot thus supports a weaker reconstructability claim
> than the thesis currently states: the harness guarantees a scoreable record, but
> freestyle can also become reconstructable when the protocol forces stronger durable
> capture.

This section should state clearly that T3 preserves parity rather than reopening the
H/F gap. That is the decisive pilot result for C3.

## C4 - Overhead

Suggested paragraph skeleton:

> C4 is the most stable pilot result so far. T1 and T2 both show high relative overhead
> for the harness condition in time, interaction volume, and artifact/process surface.
> T1 leaves some ambiguity about how much of that cost came from post-target extension
> work, but T2 shows that the overhead remains high even after the protocol is cleaned
> up around a shared stop line. T3 strengthens that reading again, with roughly `9.94x`
> scored wall-clock asymmetry and much heavier interaction volume for H. The paper
> should report high harness overhead as a stable pilot finding rather than as a
> nuisance variable.

Do not soften this section. The thesis already commits to overhead as part of the
claim, so unfavorable cost is part of the result.

## Cross-claim synthesis

Suggested paragraph skeleton:

> Across the completed pilot pairs, the strongest current result is not a simple overall
> superiority claim for the harness. It is that protocol design changes the comparative
> story. Weak baseline capture can make freestyle look less evidence-disciplined and less
> reconstructable than it really is, while stronger capture symmetry can remove much of
> those apparent advantages. At the same time, the harness still guarantees explicit
> process externalization and continues to impose substantial overhead.

## Strong-path decision paragraph

Use this ending direction after T3:

- the current evidence supports only a weaker claim than the strong path in [thesis.md](thesis.md),
- write the pilot as a methodological correction against overclaiming rather than as preliminary confirmation of the full thesis,
- make explicit that the most stable positive result is overhead, not a broad H advantage on C1-C3.

## Final assembly note

Keep updating this scaffold in place before drafting any broader results prose elsewhere.
That keeps the manuscript aligned with the atomic evidence documents instead of letting
a separate narrative drift ahead of the scored record.
